# Data Processing Agreement

This agreement forms part of the [Terms of Use](TERMS.md) between [FULL LEGAL NAME], a sole
proprietor trading as gradientERP, [MAILING ADDRESS] (the **processor**) and the business that holds a gradientERP account
(the **controller**). It covers the personal data the controller puts in its ERP instance. The
account holder's own data is covered by the [Privacy Notice](PRIVACY.md).

It applies the EU GDPR, the UK GDPR, the Swiss FADP and US state privacy laws (as a service
provider or processor) to the extent each applies. Where it conflicts with the Terms on personal
data, this agreement prevails; where the standard contractual clauses apply, they prevail over it.

## 1. The processing

| | |
|---|---|
| subject matter | hosting and running the controller's ERP instance |
| duration | the life of the account, then the closure period in section 9 |
| nature and purpose | storing, computing and presenting the controller's records; running the controller's agent over them at the controller's direction; exporting them |
| personal data | names, contact details, addresses, tax ids, payroll and payment records, and anything else the controller records — which may include special categories (for example health data in leave records), for which the controller holds the lawful basis |
| data subjects | the controller's employees, contractors, customers, vendors and other contacts |

## 2. Instructions

The processor processes the personal data only on the controller's documented instructions — the
Terms, this agreement, and what the controller directs through the service, including with regard to
transfers — unless the law requires otherwise, in which case it tells the controller first where the law allows. It tells the
controller if it believes an instruction breaks data protection law.

## 3. Confidentiality

Everyone the processor authorises to handle the personal data is bound to confidentiality.

## 4. Security

The processor keeps the measures GDPR Article 32 requires, including: each controller's instance in
its own AWS account; encryption in transit and at rest; access through audited, least-privilege
roles; and logging of administrative access.

## 5. Where the data is

The instance runs in the AWS region the controller picks, in the controller's own AWS account.
Nothing is copied out of it except the exports the controller asks for. The processor, and anyone
it authorises, reaches it from the United States, which is a transfer.

Where personal data is transferred out of the EEA, the European Commission's standard contractual
clauses (Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914), module two (controller to processor), are
incorporated by reference, with: clause 7 (docking) not used; clause 9(a) option 2 (general written
authorisation, with the notice period in section 6); clause 11 optional language not used; clause
13, the supervisory authority of the member state where the controller is established or, if none,
of the processor's EU representative; clause 17 option 1, the law of Ireland; clause 18, the courts
of Ireland. Annex I is section 1 and the parties above; Annex II is section 4; Annex III is section 6.
For the UK, the ICO's International Data Transfer Addendum applies to those clauses; for
Switzerland, they apply with the FDPIC's amendments (the FADP in place of the GDPR, the FDPIC as
supervisory authority, Swiss courts for Swiss data subjects).

## 6. Subprocessors

The controller authorises these subprocessors:

| subprocessor | purpose |
|---|---|
| Amazon Web Services, Inc. | hosting, storage, email (Amazon SES), the agent's model (Amazon Bedrock) |

Each is bound by data protection terms at least as protective as this agreement. The processor
gives notice of a new subprocessor at least 30 days before it is used; the controller may object,
and if the objection cannot be resolved, may close its account. Services the controller connects
itself — its own payment processor, bank feed or other vendors — are the controller's own vendors
under their own terms.

## 7. Helping the controller

Taking into account the nature of the processing, the processor helps the controller:

- answer requests from data subjects exercising their rights. The controller can access, correct
  and export its records itself, and erase a person as a shell — their name and contact details
  removed, the accounting entries that reference them kept, since balances are computed from that
  history and accounting law requires it be kept
- meet its obligations on security, breach notification, data protection impact assessments and
  prior consultation (GDPR Articles 32 to 36)

## 8. Breaches

The processor notifies the controller at the account email without undue delay, and within 48
hours, after becoming aware of a personal data breach affecting the controller's data, with the
nature of the breach, the categories and approximate numbers concerned, its likely consequences and
the measures taken, as far as it knows them then, and more as it learns it.

## 9. End of processing

When the account closes, the controller's records are exported to it and remain downloadable for 15
days, after which the instance and everything in it is deleted (the Terms, "Your records are yours
to keep"). The processor keeps no copy, except where the law requires it.

## 10. US state privacy laws

Where the California Consumer Privacy Act or a like US state law applies, the processor is the
controller's service provider or processor and: does not sell or share the personal data; does not
retain, use or disclose it outside the direct business relationship or for any purpose but
performing the service; does not combine it with personal data from other sources except as those
laws permit; complies with those laws and gives the same level of protection they require; tells the
controller if it can no longer meet them; and lets the controller take reasonable steps to stop and
remedy unauthorised use.

## 11. Audits

The processor makes available the information needed to demonstrate compliance with this agreement,
and allows for and contributes to audits by the controller or an auditor it mandates, on reasonable
notice and at the controller's cost.
