# Terms of Use

gradientERP is an **operator-hosted** ERP: your business's books and operations run in a dedicated cloud
account we operate for you. Using the platform means agreeing to the terms below, the
[Privacy Notice](PRIVACY.md) for your account data, and the [Data Processing Agreement](DPA.md) for
the personal data your business keeps in its instance.

**The principle:** an openly-operated economy is visible. Your economic activity is part of a public
economic record. If you want your business to stay private, don't use the platform.

## What's public

- **Your aggregated economic activity — always.** The value and volume of your transactions feed anonymized,
  aggregate economic indicators published and queryable on openlyoperated.biz. This is a condition of use,
  not a toggle. The index holds sums and counts across businesses, sliced by signal, time, category, and
  place — sliced finely enough that a figure can trace back to you. That's intended, and you consent to it.
- **Your identifiable books — unless you turn it off.** The "openly operated" setting (on by default)
  publishes your business page — statements, inventory, orders, schedules — read from your own instance at
  view time. Turn it off and new public reads stop.

## What's never public

Your **people's** information — employees', contractors', and customers' names and contact details, their
legal and tax records (I-9s, W-4s, tax IDs), and your secrets and credentials — is never on any public
surface, aggregate or page. "Part of the economy" makes your business's activity visible; it does not make a
person's data visible. Those live in a separate part of your instance that public reads never touch.

## What you send other businesses

Addressed events — an invoice, a purchase order, a quote request — are handled automatically by the
receiving business's tools, because a protocol nobody has to configure first is the point. Send an
invoice and it becomes a payable on their books.

That only works while what you send is what it claims to be. **There is no advertising on this
network.** Sending a business document as a solicitation, or to a business you have no dealing with,
ends your use of the platform. We don't filter it and we don't score it; we remove the sender.

Advertising is not restricted here because it's distasteful — it's redundant. A buyer's stated need
already reaches every peer holding that stock, priced and ranked. Broadcasting competes with a
signal that is strictly better than it, and the only thing it can add is noise. Sell by being the
match.

## If you don't pay

Your gerp is billed monthly: what AWS charged to run it, plus 20%. AWS's own invoice for your
instance is what the bill is computed from, and it's kept with the entry that books it.

An unpaid invoice runs a schedule, and you hear from us at every step. Nothing happens quietly.

| days 3, 6, 9, 12 | we email you that the invoice is unpaid, what it is for, a link to pay it, and the date your gerp closes if it stays unpaid |
| day 15 | we close your gerp |
| day 15 to day 30 | your books and documents are exported and yours to download; the instance is stopped |
| day 30 | the account and everything in it is deleted |

**Paying stops the ladder at any point before day 15**, and there is nothing to restore because
nothing has been taken away yet.

**Closing is not instant deletion.** From the day an invoice goes unpaid you have fifteen days before
anything closes and thirty before anything is deleted, and the export happens first. What you do not
get is a paused instance you can resume later — a closed gerp is closed, and starting again means
starting again.

## Your records are yours to keep

Your books are your business's records, and what you are required to keep, for how long, is between
you and whoever requires it. We are not your recordkeeper and we are not liable for your cost of
compliance.

What we owe you is the copy: your books and documents are exported to you at closure, and your
account keeps running for 15 days afterward — billed — so you can take them. Keeping that export,
and keeping it somewhere that satisfies whatever rule applies to you, is yours to do. After that
window the account and everything in it is deleted, and we hold no copy to give you later.

## What we don't do

We don't keep a central copy of your data. Your public page reads through to your own instance; the economic
index holds only aggregate counters, not your individual events.
