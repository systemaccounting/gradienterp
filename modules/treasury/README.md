# treasury

## the gradient

electrons chase a gradient produced by a potential. current flows down the voltage gradient. the circuit obeys conservation at every node — kirchhoff.

capital does the same thing. the potential is the published margin: `(r - e) / e`. the current is capital flow. resistance is information asymmetry — opaque books, quarterly reports, auditor lag. reduce the resistance, the current flows faster, equilibrium is reached sooner.

publishing `(r - e) / e` in real time creates the potential field. capital doesn't need analysts, insider tips, or earnings calls. it reads the gradient directly from the event stream and moves.

## conservation

double-entry accounting isn't a metaphor for kirchhoff. it IS kirchhoff. every journal entry is a vector that sums to zero — conservation holds at every node, at every instant. the accounting module enforces this at write time.

the treasury module publishes the gradient that conservation produces. revenue in, expenses out, the difference is the potential. visible to everyone, continuously.

## demand locates supply

capital seeks return. that's demand. return is produced by businesses converting inputs into outputs at a margin. that's supply.

in opaque markets, demand can't locate the empirical supply of return because the numbers are hidden, delayed, or fabricated. the information asymmetry between capital and return creates resistance — mispricing, misallocation, friction.

publishing the gradient removes the resistance. capital sees where returns are above average and flows there directly.

## dynamic equilibrium

when capital finds above-average return, it flows in. the new capital funds competition — more capacity, more r&d, better processes. competition pushes prices down. margins compress toward the mean. capital moves on to the next above-average return.

this is the equilibrium mechanism economics describes theoretically but can't execute because the data isn't there. the theoretical assumptions — perfect information, symmetric access, rational flow — are infrastructure problems, not behavioral ones. openly-operated businesses on gradienterp provide the infrastructure.

modigliani-miller assumed symmetric information and concluded capital structure is irrelevant. openly-operated businesses make the assumption hold empirically. debt and equity collapse to the same thing: an automation rule paying a holder from a visible margin.

## the cap table is stock-free

the cap table here is not a register of shares. it's the set of active distribution rules — who is owed what, paid from the visible margin. there is no stock, and that is the point.

the constraint isn't the cap — it's the payout. a rule has to do something, or it isn't a rule. "pay 10% of net income until $750k is returned" does something; so does "pay 5% of net income for as long as you hold it," uncapped, with no end date. both are fine, both are tradeable. and either way the value is anchored: capital prices the rule on the distributions it will actually produce, read straight off the live margin — not on what a greater fool will pay later.

a "share" is the degenerate case: a claim that pays nothing. its only return is resale to the next holder, its only other right a vote. the system can't create it — there's no such thing as a rule that does nothing. and what makes that dangerous isn't open-ended upside (an uncapped rule has that too) — it's that with no payout to anchor it, value is pure expectation, "someday this pays off," and that expectation is the thing analysts arbitrage. price chases price, the loop runs boom to bust detached from the gradient, the equity market becomes a resale casino. a non-paying claim is exactly where shares turn into speculation and political theater.

strip the vote, too. stock bundles a financial claim with control — boards, proxy fights, activists pressing the owner to juice the quarter at the expense of the business. a distribution rule carries no vote; it's a claim on a payout, nothing more. the transparent owner runs the business in peace. the investor's lever is not voice, it's exit: if the return disappoints, move on — and because the margin is visible in real time, exit is fully informed. capital reads the gradient and flows. it doesn't sit on the board and meddle.

discipline by flow, not by governance. that's the whole mechanism: capital enters where the return is above average and leaves where it isn't, and the leaving is enough. nothing created that pays nothing, no seats to contest — just rules paying holders from a margin everyone can see.

## negentropy

capital that flows to above-average returns and competes them down is doing thermodynamic work — reducing entropy in the price system. every dollar of margin compressed is a dollar of waste removed. prices land on one row toward cost.

a dividend paid as a lower price to the customer is a permanent efficiency gain. it doesn't get taxed as income. it doesn't get reinvested in speculation. it reduces the cost of living directly.

money that ships dividends through price decreases is negentropic. the gradient is visible. capital responds. prices fall. the cycle repeats.

## the marketplace
